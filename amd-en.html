I. Our Views

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Data Center Revenue More Than Doubled, Driving Record Quarterly Revenue; AI Accelerators and Server CPUs Form a Dual Growth Engine

    Q2 2026 revenue reached $11,536M (+50% YoY / +13% QoQ), a company-disclosed quarterly record and the sixth consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth above 30%. Data Center revenue was $6,718M (+107% YoY, a quarterly record), lifting its share of total revenue to 58% from 42% a year ago. Management noted EPYC sales grew more than 70% YoY (with record enterprise sell-through) while Instinct accelerator revenue more than doubled. We believe AI demand is now pulling the accelerator and server-CPU growth curves in parallel, shifting the revenue structure from a single product cycle to a dual-engine model.

  • 2.
    Helios Rack-Scale Shipments Begin This Quarter; Three Frontier-AI Anchor Customers Underpin the 2027 Ramp

    AMD launched the Helios rack-scale AI platform this quarter (integrating EPYC Venice CPUs, Instinct MI450-series GPUs, Pensando networking and ROCm software), with initial shipments slated to start in Q3 2026 and scale from Q4 2026. Beyond existing gigawatt-scale programs with OpenAI and Meta, AMD added a strategic partnership with Anthropic this quarter (up to 2 GW of MI450-series GPUs in Helios racks, with the first gigawatt deploying from the first half of 2027) and expanded its Microsoft collaboration to deploy Helios at scale on Azure. Management expects server revenue to grow more than 80% YoY in the second half of 2026 and Data Center segment revenue to more than double in 2027. We believe order visibility has improved materially, though the pace of the ramp still hinges on customers' data-center build-out schedules.

  • 3.
    Profitability Upgraded in Tandem: Comparable-Basis Gross Margin and EPS Expansion, Record Cash Position

    Non-GAAP gross margin was 56% (up more than 200bps YoY on a comparable basis, driven by a higher Data Center revenue mix), and non-GAAP operating income reached a quarterly record of $3,094M (27% margin). Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $1.66 (a quarterly record, up 82% YoY on a comparable basis). Quarter-end cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments reached a record $13,111M, with quarterly free cash flow of $1,558M (14% margin). AMD guided Q3 2026 revenue to approximately $13.0B (± $300M, roughly +41% YoY at the midpoint) with a non-GAAP gross margin of approximately 56%. (The comparable basis excludes the $800M of inventory and related charges recognized in Q2 2025 due to U.S. export controls.)

  • 4.
    Consensus Target Implies Roughly +28.2% Upside; the Valuation Premium Sits Within the AI-Semiconductor Peer Range

    Based on the 08/12/2026 closing price of US$482.93, the Bloomberg ANR consensus target of US$619.33 implies roughly +28.2% upside; the consensus rating is Buy (Buys 81.8% / Holds 18.2% / Sells 0.0%). On next-four-quarters estimated EPS the stock trades at 43.16x — above NVDA at 22.42x and AVGO at 26.44x, and below INTC at 62.35x (all four on Bloomberg EEO adjusted-EPS conventions for a consistent basis). We believe the premium reflects market expectations for the 2027 Helios/MI450 ramp; any shortfall in the shipment ramp, yield improvement or customer data-center readiness could amplify share-price volatility through multiple compression.

IV. Analyst Views

Morgan Stanley

Neutral
  • Neutral Rating (PT $465): Against fundamentals of 107% YoY Data Center growth and the start of Helios shipments this quarter, the rating sits in neutral territory, reflecting a balanced risk/reward assessment at current levels.
  • Earnings-Call Questions Focused on Server-CPU Supply and New-Node Ramp: Its analyst probed the persistence of server-CPU supply tightness and the execution challenge of ramping the 2nm node; management responded that 2027 server supply should be better than 2026 and that the chiplet approach reduces wafer requirements on new nodes.

UBS

Buy
  • Buy Rating (PT $730): The constructive stance aligns with order visibility — beyond existing gigawatt-scale programs with OpenAI and Meta, AMD added Anthropic (up to 2 GW of MI450-series GPUs) and a scaled Helios deployment on Microsoft Azure this quarter, and management expects Data Center segment revenue to more than double in 2027.
  • Earnings-Call Questions Focused on Data Center Growth Cadence and Long-Term TAM: Its analyst pressed on the Q3/Q4 growth mix between server CPUs and data-center AI, and on whether AMD can grow faster than the ~40% market CAGR; management explicitly responded that company revenue growth will exceed the market's.

JP Morgan

Neutral
  • Neutral Rating (PT $550): The stance reflects a balance between strong momentum and a valuation premium — Data Center revenue grew 107% YoY and Helios shipments begin this quarter, while the stock trades at 43.16x next-four-quarters consensus EPS versus 22.42x for NVDA and 26.44x for AVGO; excess returns depend on the ramp being delivered on schedule.
  • Key Watch Items: Helios Ramp and Margin Verification: Initial Helios shipments start in Q3 2026 and scale from Q4 2026; management noted early yields are still improving and that data-center-AI gross margin is slightly below the corporate average — both are the key variables to monitor in coming quarters.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Export Controls and Geopolitical Risk: U.S. export controls on Instinct MI308 data-center GPUs led to $800 million of inventory and related charges in Q2 2025; further changes in export rules, tariffs or licensing requirements could still affect the addressable scope and profitability of specific product lines.
  2. Supply-Chain and Memory Cost Pressure: Server-CPU supply remained tight through the first half of 2026, and the Helios ramp depends on 2nm capacity, advanced packaging, substrates and HBM availability; management also noted that rising memory and component costs are weighing on PC and graphics-card demand and expects a softer PC market in the second half.
  3. Customer Concentration and Data-Center Readiness: The 2027 data-center-AI growth outlook relies heavily on gigawatt-scale deployments by frontier-AI customers such as OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic and their cloud partners; delays in customers' land, power or rack readiness would affect shipment and revenue-recognition timing.
  4. Competition and Product-Execution Risk: Competition in AI accelerators and server CPUs is intense; Helios is a highly complex rack-scale system whose early yields are still ramping, while the Gaming business (-31% YoY in Q2 2026) remains a drag late in the console cycle.
