I. Our Views

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Earnings Inflection Established; Full-Year Guidance Raised Sharply

    2Q26 adjusted EPS reached $6.38 (up 56.4% YoY) with earnings from operations of $7,991 million (up 55.2% YoY). Management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance from above $17.75 to a range of $19.50–$20.00, and Group operating earnings guidance to above $25.45 billion; we believe the pace of rebuilding market trust is running ahead of expectations.

  • 2.
    Marked MCR Improvement, with Divergence Across Books

    The 2Q26 medical care ratio (MCR) of 86.7% improved 270 bps from 89.4% a year ago, aided by benefit design, pricing discipline and $860 million of net favorable prior period medical reserve development (the majority related to 2026 dates of service). Medicare cost trend is running below the initial ~10% assumption, but commercial trend has edged above 11% and the Medicaid rate gap remains unresolved — full-year MCR guidance of 88.1% ±25 bps implies cost pressure persists into 2H.

  • 3.
    Optum Back on a Growth Track with Broad Margin Expansion

    Optum's quarterly earnings from operations of $4,049 million rose 31.7% YoY, with margin up 160 bps to 6.2%: Optum Health re-centered on integrated value-based care with margin recovering to 5.1%; Optum Insight posted a 25.3% margin and closed the Alegeus acquisition on July 2; Optum Rx's transparent fee-based model is resonating with clients, with retention holding in the high 90s.

  • 4.
    Capital Returns Stepped Up; Room for Valuation Recovery

    Quarterly cash flows from operations were $11.1 billion (1.9x net income); the full-year buyback target was doubled from $2.5 billion to at least $5.0 billion, the annualized dividend was raised to $9.28, and debt-to-capital fell to 41.2%, tracking toward ~40% by year-end. Based on the 07/16/2026 closing price of $423.38, the Bloomberg ANR consensus target of $455.08 implies ~7.5% potential upside; we believe valuation has room to recover provided guidance is delivered.

IV. Analyst Views

Summary of top Wall Street investment banks' takes on UNH's 2Q26 earnings inflection and guidance raise (ratings and price targets: Bloomberg ANR, as of 07/17/2026):

Morgan Stanley

Overweight
  • Guidance Raise Validates Execution Discipline (PT $468): 2Q26 adjusted EPS of $6.38 far exceeded the $4.08 of a year ago, and full-year guidance was raised to a range of $19.50–$20.00 — evidence that management's "more consistent and dependable" operating discipline is being delivered.
  • Medicare Advantage as the Core Recovery Engine: The 86.7% MCR improved markedly from 89.4% a year ago, with Medicare cost trend running below the initial ~10% assumption; benefit design and network management gains can support further margin improvement into 2027.

UBS

Buy
  • Estimate Upgrades Drive Re-Rating (PT $460): Following a strong 2Q beat, full-year adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $19.50–$20.00; at 21.47x next-4-quarters estimated earnings, upward estimate revisions form the key driver of share re-rating.
  • Capital Returns Stepped Up Across the Board: The full-year buyback target was doubled from $2.5 billion to at least $5.0 billion, the annualized dividend was raised to $9.28, and debt-to-capital fell to 41.2% — balance-sheet repair enhances the visibility of shareholder returns.

Barclays

Overweight
  • A Relatively Cautious Overweight (PT $429): The target sits below the ANR consensus of $455.08, reflecting commercial cost trend edging above 11% and a Medicaid rate gap unlikely to close near term; the margin-recovery cadence still requires quarter-by-quarter validation.
  • Optum Recovery Is Key to Further Re-Rating: Optum Health's margin recovered to 5.1%, though value-based care patients fell by ~700,000 YoY; progress on 2027 rate negotiations and scale rebuilding will determine the pace and magnitude of re-rating.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Commercial Medical Cost Trend Shows No Moderation: Commercial cost trend is running modestly above the prior 11% level. The No Surprises Act independent dispute resolution process (applicable only to commercial plans) and providers' higher coding intensity and cost per encounter create persistent pressure; management concedes commercial margin recovery will take longer than originally anticipated.
  2. Government-Program Policy & Regulatory Uncertainty: DOJ legal actions concerning participation in the Medicare program, changes to CMS star ratings and risk adjustment data validation (RADV) audits, and Medicaid rates and eligibility redeterminations could all materially affect revenues and earnings.
  3. Shrinking Membership Weighs on Revenue Momentum: Full-year Medicare Advantage enrollment is expected to decline by ~1.1 million; Optum Health value-based care patients fell by ~700,000; and Optum Rx quarterly adjusted scripts declined 6.5% YoY (387M vs 414M). Slower-than-expected retention and rate improvements would keep revenue growth under pressure.
  4. Transformation Investments Lift Costs; Execution Risk: The 2Q26 operating cost ratio rose to 12.7% (12.3% a year ago), reflecting continued investment in technology, AI and care experience; the timing and magnitude of returns on large-scale transformation and AI adoption remain uncertain.
