I. Our Views

Based on the 2Q2026 earnings results and related disclosure documents, the following are the core conclusions distilled by the NTAM research team on Johnson & Johnson (JNJ):

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Innovative Medicine's dual engines offset the impact of key patent expiry

    Although STELARA faces biosimilar competition (a drag of approximately 760 basis points on Innovative Medicine's operational growth), the segment still delivered solid worldwide operational sales growth of 6.8%, supported by the multiple myeloma therapy DARZALEX (operational growth +17.6%), CAR-T therapy CARVYKTI (+47.7%), and TREMFYA (+71.0%).

  • 2.
    MedTech maintains steady growth; Shockwave sustains double-digit expansion

    MedTech recorded quarterly sales of US$8.926 billion, with operational growth of 3.6%. Cardiovascular operational growth was 3.1%, within which the newly acquired Shockwave Intravascular Lithotripsy (IVL) system grew 14.7% operationally, supported by adoption across coronary and peripheral products and new launches; Electrophysiology grew 3.1% operationally, aided by the VARIPULSE platform launch in Europe.

  • 3.
    Company raises 2026 sales and adjusted EPS guidance

    Supported by first-half operational performance, management raised its full-year 2026 reported sales guidance to US$100.8–101.4 billion (midpoint US$101.1 billion, up 7.3% year-over-year), and raised the Adjusted Reported EPS guidance midpoint to US$11.68. Based on current guidance, the company is on track to surpass US$100 billion in annual sales for the first time.

  • 4.
    R&D investment remains high; capital returns stay stable

    The company invested approximately US$3.7 billion in R&D this quarter (around 14.4% of sales), continuing to advance a late-stage clinical pipeline including nipocalimab. A diversified business mix helps reduce dependence on any single product, while stable cash flow reflects the company's ability to maintain consistent capital returns.

IV. Analyst Views

A summary of leading Wall Street banks' views on Johnson & Johnson's long-term strategy (Ratings and price targets: Bloomberg ANR, as of 07/16/2026):

Morgan Stanley

Overweight
  • Resilient pipeline defense (PT: $294.00): STELARA biosimilar competition is an expected headwind, but DARZALEX share gains and CARVYKTI's continued capacity ramp strengthen J&J's competitive positioning in oncology.
  • Guidance raise supports valuation: The company narrowed and raised its full-year sales and EPS guidance midpoints amid macro uncertainty, reflecting the resilience of its diversified healthcare mix.

UBS

Buy
  • Acquired assets contribute to growth (PT: $280.00): Shockwave delivered 14.7% operational growth within Cardiovascular; as the VARIPULSE electrophysiology platform rolls out, acquired assets are beginning to contribute to revenue growth.
  • R&D reinvestment at industry-high levels: With roughly US$3.7 billion invested in R&D this quarter, the late-stage pipeline (e.g., nipocalimab in autoimmune disease) is expected to deliver catalysts across 2026–2027.

Citi

Buy
  • Approaching the US$100bn milestone (PT: $298.00): Quarterly sales exceeded US$25 billion; based on current guidance, full-year sales are on track to surpass US$100 billion for the first time, offering relative defensiveness amid market volatility.
  • Stable capital returns: With 1H26 estimated free cash flow of approximately US$8.7 billion, a strong balance sheet supports a stable dividend, providing downside protection for long-term institutional shareholders.

V. Risk Factors

  1. STELARA biosimilar erosion risk: Biosimilar competition from patent expiry has already caused an approximately 760 basis-point drag on Innovative Medicine's operational growth this quarter. If the ramp of newer products lags expectations, segment margins may come under pressure.
  2. Execution friction from the planned Orthopaedics separation: The company is advancing the planned separation of its Orthopaedics business; management and execution friction during restructuring and supply-chain adjustment could create near-term uncertainty for MedTech shipments and capacity.
  3. Margin pressure from international procurement policy and surgical transition: China's volume-based procurement (VBP) policy continues to exert pricing and competitive pressure on MedTech's surgical devices, and the related business transition remains in a near-term adjustment phase.
  4. Potential liability uncertainty from legacy talc litigation: Legacy talc product litigation and the scale of potential settlements remain a long-term uncertainty on the balance sheet, and may be subject to further regulatory scrutiny.
