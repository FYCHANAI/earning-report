I. Our Views

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Look through the base effect and the lower share count

    We believe Citi’s earnings recovery is meaningful, but the 53% EPS increase overstates the underlying operating pace. Net income reached US$5.245B and diluted EPS US$2.85. Excluding the prior-year Banamex impairment, net-income growth was 17% and EPS growth 27%; the gap also reflects share repurchases. Quarterly buybacks of US$3.0B support per-share earnings, but should be distinguished from growth in the business itself.

  • 2.
    Services and Wealth strengthen the return mix

    We believe Services and Wealth are becoming more credible engines of sustainable returns. Both grew revenue 15%, to US$6.614B and US$3.269B, with RoTCE of 31.2% and 14.3%. Services average deposits rose 16%; Wealth attracted US$38.1B of net new investment assets. These client relationships broaden earnings beyond Markets, although deposit spreads and asset-market levels still influence results.

  • 3.
    Efficiency is improving, but progress is not linear

    We believe the restructuring is delivering benefits without being a finished story. The efficiency ratio improved to 58.5% from 64.7% a year ago, but prior-year impairment inflated that comparison; expenses rose 3% excluding the item. Quarterly RoTCE of 11.5% fell from 13.0%, while the year-to-date figure was 12.6%. Guidance for above-11% full-year RoTCE and a below-60% efficiency ratio requires continued expense discipline alongside investment.

  • 4.
    A low forward multiple still requires sustained returns

    We believe 9.46x FY2027 Bloomberg Adj+ EPS offers scope for re-rating if returns improve sustainably. However, US$122.27 is already about 1.20x latest tangible book, rather than a discount to it. CET1 of 12.8% provides about 120bp above the current requirement; US$4.1B was returned to common shareholders. Buybacks above tangible book can support EPS while diluting TBVPS. The broad ANR target implies 23.6% price upside and is not a post-results-only consensus.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Card economics and credit costs U.S. Consumer Cards revenue was flat despite loan growth. Higher partner payments and rewards reduced non-interest revenue to negative US$758M. The segment added US$208M of reserves; unemployment deterioration could offset lower current charge-offs.
  2. Transformation costs and the path to returns The efficiency ratio worsened sequentially from 57.4% to 58.5%. Investment, execution delays or weaker revenues can slow progress toward higher returns; above-11% 2026 RoTCE guidance is not a guarantee of the longer-term 14–15% ambition.
  3. Global markets, currencies and funding Cross-border deposits, trading and Wealth fees depend on client activity and market conditions. Higher rates, inflation, geopolitical conflict and emerging-market currency moves can affect revenue, funding costs and credit quality simultaneously.
  4. Capital, Banamex and per-share trade-offs Citi retains 51% of Banamex. Further exits depend on execution and market conditions, while capital returns compete with growth and regulatory needs. At above tangible book, buybacks may lift EPS but dilute TBVPS; the 12.8% CET1 ratio and 5.0% SLR must be monitored together.
