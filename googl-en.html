I. Our Views

Key Operating Conclusions

  • 1.
    Double-digit Search growth supports the AI monetization case

    Google Search & other revenue rose 12% to US$63.35bn, while Google Services operating income reached US$39.16bn. We believe Search remains Alphabet's primary earnings foundation. AI features that sustain query growth and advertising conversion can reinforce its commercial value. The key question is whether incremental revenue offsets higher inference costs, rather than user growth alone.

  • 2.
    Cloud is becoming a larger growth and earnings contributor

    Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to US$30.71bn, with US$11.12bn operating income and a 36.2% margin. We believe the combination of scale and profitability provides tangible evidence of demand for AI infrastructure and enterprise solutions. TPU system sales also contributed. Investors should monitor the mix between hardware sales and recurring cloud services, and the durability of this growth.

  • 3.
    Operating progress faces a higher capital-intensity hurdle

    Operating income grew approximately 30%, but earnings available to common shareholders fell around 25% as other income turned into expense. Operating cash flow of US$42.29bn was below US$51.36bn of capital expenditure, resulting in negative US$9.07bn free cash flow. We believe returns on new compute capacity are now central to the investment case: operating growth has not yet offset accelerated investment's pressure on free cash flow.

  • 4.
    A valuation discount offers potential, but cash returns must support rerating

    Bloomberg's 20.22x next-four-quarter P/E is below the selected Amazon, Microsoft and Apple multiples. The US$402.59 consensus target implies approximately 41.8% price upside from the 10/29 reference price of US$283.82. We believe Search resilience and Cloud growth support long-term value, but the discount also reflects capital spending, regulation and volatile investment returns. Target-price upside should be assessed alongside cash conversion and earnings quality, not treated as a guaranteed return.

V. Risk Factors

  1. AI investment and cash returns：Capital expenditure is growing faster than operating cash flow, potentially prolonging pressure on free cash flow. Utilization, pricing and depreciation of new capacity will determine returns.
  2. Search competition and monetization costs：Generative AI and alternative search channels may change user behavior and advertising models. Core margins could weaken if inference costs grow faster than revenue.
  3. Cloud growth and revenue mix：Large AI projects and TPU system sales may create quarterly volatility. Different margins across hardware, infrastructure and software services will influence future profitability.
  4. Investment effects, funding and regulation：Investment gains and losses can materially affect net income and EPS. Common-share and debt issuance warrant attention to dilution and capital allocation. Antitrust, privacy and AI regulation may also affect operations and costs.
