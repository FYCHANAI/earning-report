I. Our Views

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Cloud Reaches 60% of Revenue as OCI and Software Trends Diverge

    Q1 revenue was US$19.345bn, up 30% YoY. Cloud revenue grew 62% to US$11.607bn, or 60.0% of total revenue: OCI generated US$7.388bn, up 121%, and SaaS US$4.219bn, up 10%; software revenue declined 3%. The non-GAAP operating margin was 42.1%. We believe cloud is the growth engine, but infrastructure costs, applications growth and the transition of legacy software also matter. OCI growth does not automatically translate into higher margins across the business.

  • 2.
    US$664bn RPO Provides Visibility, but Revenue Depends on Delivery

    Remaining performance obligations (RPO) reached US$664bn, US$209bn above the prior year. Only 13% is expected to be recognized as revenue within the next 12 months. The company delivered 850MW of additional capacity during Q1 and disclosed deliveries of more than 300,000 GPUs since the end of Q4 FY2026. We believe the backlog improves demand visibility. RPO represents contracted revenue not yet recognized, not a measure of cash collected; some contracts may be prepaid. Data-center commissioning, customer performance and collection timing determine its conversion into earnings.

  • 3.
    Customer Prepayments Support Cash Flow; Capital Needs Remain Substantial

    Q1 operating cash flow of US$23.103bn included US$11.363bn of customer prepayments with a significant financing component. Capital expenditure was US$28.499bn, leaving free cash flow negative at US$5.396bn. The company also raised US$20bn gross through an ATM common-stock offering, issuing 141 million shares. We believe prepayments improve construction funding, but all operating cash flow cannot be treated as recurring cash earnings, nor do they eliminate financing and dilution pressures.

  • 4.
    Q2 Guidance Signals Growth; Valuation Requires Comparable Earnings and Cash Conversion

    Q2 guidance calls for USD revenue growth of 30–34%, cloud growth of 65–71% and non-GAAP EPS of US$1.85–1.93. The 21–25% EPS growth comparison excludes the prior-year one-time Ampere gain; retaining that gain produces a decline of 14–18%. FY2027 guidance is at least US$90bn in revenue and US$8.10 non-GAAP EPS. Bloomberg shows 16.75x next-four-quarter P/E. The ANR consensus target of US$240.01 implies 67.2% upside from its displayed reference price of US$143.56, excluding dividends. We believe delivery, cash collection and per-share earnings must improve together to support the valuation.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Data-Center Delivery, Power and GPU Supply Risk: RPO converts into revenue as contractual obligations are fulfilled; cloud infrastructure contracts also require capacity to come online on schedule. Constraints in GPUs, power, networking, cooling or construction can delay service delivery and raise costs. Delivered capacity does not imply that all of it immediately generates revenue.
  2. Capital Requirements, Funding and Dilution Risk: Q1 capex of US$28.499bn exceeded operating cash flow and FCF remained negative. The timing and persistence of customer prepayments also affect cash flow. The ATM offering issued 141 million shares; further capital needs may increase financing costs or dilute per-share returns. The company expects FY2027 capex above FY2026, making the pace of investment and funding a key monitoring point.
  3. Customer Credit and Long-Term Contract Performance Risk: Most of the US$664bn RPO spans multiple years, with only 13% expected to be recognized in the next 12 months. Customer funding, actual compute demand, payment and performance arrangements can change revenue timing. The entire backlog cannot be treated as near-term revenue or available cash.
  4. Competition, Cybersecurity and Regulatory Risk: Competition in cloud services and enterprise applications may constrain pricing. OCI growth comes with higher infrastructure costs, and margins do not necessarily improve across all businesses. Cyber incidents, outages, privacy rules and healthcare regulation may raise compensation and compliance costs or lead to customer losses.
