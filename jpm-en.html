I. Our Views

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Recurring income is the stronger foundation

    We believe deposit and loan growth provide a firmer earnings base than the headline sequential decline suggests. Managed revenue rose 8% to US$50.907B and NII excluding Markets rose 6% to US$24.7B. Q4 guidance is about US$25.5B for NII excluding Markets, while the preliminary 2027 central case is about US$107B. Reported EPS of US$5.57 and company ex-item EPS of US$5.75 should be kept distinct; Q2 investment gains distort sequential comparisons.

  • 2.
    Fee franchises broaden growth, but trading is uneven

    We believe the 14% revenue growth in both CIB and AWM demonstrates the value of the diversified franchise. AWM generated US$51B of long-term and US$42B of liquidity net inflows. CIB equity-markets revenue rose 49%, yet fixed-income revenue fell 4%. Payments revenue grew 14%, offering a more recurring counterweight to trading volatility; an equally strong trading quarter should not be assumed.

  • 3.
    Credit is resilient; cost discipline is the next test

    We believe credit performance supports earnings, but revenue growth alone does not guarantee better efficiency. Card net charge-offs improved to 2.91% from 3.15% a year earlier and group provisions fell to US$2.743B from US$3.403B. Nevertheless, expenses rose 12% against 8% managed revenue growth, and reserves increased US$539M. Technology, marketing and compensation investment must translate into future revenue or productivity gains.

  • 4.
    Quality commands a premium and creates a capital trade-off

    We believe 20% reported ROTCE helps explain JPM’s valuation premium, but leaves less room for execution slips. The shares trade at 13.20x FY2027 Bloomberg Adj+ EPS and about 2.98x latest tangible book. Standardized CET1 fell to 13.9% from 14.2%; net repurchases were US$664M as management prioritized franchise investment. The broad ANR target implies 10.9% price upside, but includes estimates made before and after earnings.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Rates and balance-sheet sensitivity Higher rates support some reinvestment yields but can depress securities values and increase funding costs. The 2027 US$107B NII ex-Markets central case is preliminary and depends on balance-sheet growth and rates; it should not be treated as secured earnings.
  2. Cost growth and revenue cyclicality Expenses rose 12%, while fixed-income revenue declined 4%. Weaker capital-markets activity or falling asset values could slow fee growth before technology, compensation and marketing costs adjust.
  3. Credit deterioration beyond the current cycle A 2.91% card charge-off rate is reassuring, but US$539M of additional reserves and wholesale credit costs show that risk has not disappeared. Employment weakness, concentrated corporate exposures or geopolitical shocks could raise losses.
  4. Capital use and valuation compression CET1 fell 30bp sequentially and buybacks were reduced. Loan growth, risk-weighted assets, securities valuations and regulation can compete for capital; a roughly 2.98x tangible-book valuation magnifies sensitivity to a lower sustainable ROTCE.
