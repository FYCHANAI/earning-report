I. Our Views

Based on 2Q26 earnings results and related disclosure documents, the following are the core conclusions distilled by the NTAM research team on JPMorgan Chase (JPM):

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Record Revenue Across All Three Lines of Business; Corporate Lifted by Significant Items

    2Q26 net income reached $21.2 billion (EPS $7.70, +47% YoY) on managed revenue of $58.0 billion, up 27% YoY. Revenue in each of the three lines of business (CCB, CIB, AWM) hit a new record, while Corporate's $6.05 billion in revenue was primarily lifted by the Visa share net gain (+$1.27 EPS) and equity investment gains (+$0.29 EPS). Excluding significant items, core net income was $16.9 billion (EPS $6.14, ROTCE 23%, revenue +15%) — core performance remained strong, though the quarter also benefited from notably improved market activity and trading conditions.

  • 2.
    CIB Firing on Both Engines: Markets and IB Fees Surged in Tandem

    Markets revenue of $12.1 billion rose 35% YoY, led by an 86% jump in Equity Markets; IB fees of $3.28 billion climbed 30% YoY to the highest quarterly level since 2021, holding the #1 global IB fee ranking with a 9.3% wallet share (Dealogic, as of 7/1/2026). Management noted that market sentiment remains constructive for continued activity.

  • 3.
    Fortress Balance Sheet Underpins Robust Shareholder Returns

    Standardized CET1 stood at 14.1% with Total Loss-Absorbing Capacity (TLAC) of $590 billion. The quarter saw $6.2 billion of net common stock repurchases and $4.0 billion in dividends ($1.50 per share), with an LTM net payout ratio of 73%. A new $50 billion buyback authorization took effect on July 1, and TBVPS rose 10% YoY to $113.35.

  • 4.
    Guidance Raised, Risks Coexist: Higher NII Alongside Higher Expenses

    FY2026 NII guidance was raised to ~$105.5 billion (~$96.5 billion ex-Markets), adjusted expense to ~$107.5 billion (on higher volume- and revenue-related costs), and the Card Services NCO rate to ~3.2%. Dimon also flagged accumulating "tectonic" risks: geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation, large fiscal deficits and elevated asset prices.

IV. Analyst Views

Summary of top Wall Street investment banks' takes on JPMorgan Chase's 2Q26 results (ratings & price targets: Bloomberg ANR, as of 07/15/2026):

Morgan Stanley

Neutral
  • Strong Fundamentals Largely Priced In (PT $370): 2Q26 beat expectations across all lines of business, but the stock trades at ~14.8x forward P/E, a premium to large-bank peers such as BAC and Citi, leaving relatively limited upside.
  • Expenses Rising with the Investment Cycle: FY2026 adjusted expense guidance was raised to ~$107.5 billion, mainly on higher volume- and revenue-related compensation as activity scales; sustained operating-leverage improvement remains to be seen.

Goldman Sachs

Buy
  • Durable Capital Markets Momentum (PT $418): IB fees rose 30% YoY to the highest level since 2021, holding the #1 global ranking with a 9.3% YTD wallet share (Dealogic); market sentiment remains constructive and deal pipelines stay healthy.
  • Substantial Capital-Return Capacity: CET1 of 14.1% sits well above requirements, a new $50 billion buyback authorization took effect on July 1, the LTM net payout reached 73%, and TBVPS grew 10% YoY.

UBS

Buy
  • Diversified Engines Offset Falling Rates (PT $384): NII ex-Markets still grew 4% YoY despite rate cuts, with AWM fees and Card growth cushioning deposit-margin pressure; FY2026 NII is guided at ~$105.5 billion.
  • Credit Quality Better Than Feared: Net charge-offs of $2.4 billion edged down YoY, the Card NCO rate improved QoQ to 3.34% with a ~3.2% full-year guide, and the total allowance for credit losses stands at $31.4 billion, providing ample cushion.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Overlapping "Tectonic" Macro Risks: Dimon explicitly warned that geopolitical tensions and wars, sticky inflation, large global fiscal deficits and elevated asset prices are shifting beneath the surface like tectonic plates; a collision could trigger meaningful market disruption, and the firm cannot predict how these forces will ultimately play out.
  2. High Base and Volatility of Capital Markets Revenue: 2Q26 Markets revenue (+35%, Equities +86%) and IB fees (+30%) benefited from a "particularly favorable environment" and elevated activity; such revenue is highly cyclical and should not be extrapolated linearly into future quarters.
  3. Lower Rates Pressure Parts of the Net Interest Margin: Corporate NII fell $667 million YoY on lower rates, though higher deposits, Card revolving balances and wholesale loans supported firmwide NII growth of 10% (+4% ex-Markets). Should policy rates fall faster than management assumes, deposit margins and ALM income could face further pressure.
  4. Credit Normalization and Consumer Risk: The quarter saw a $149 million net reserve build (mainly Wholesale) with a ~3.2% full-year Card NCO guide; a softening labor market could push consumer credit losses higher and lift provisioning needs.
