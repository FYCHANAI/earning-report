I. Our Views

Based on 2Q26 earnings results and related disclosure documents, the following are the core conclusions distilled by the NTAM research team on Bank of America (BAC):

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Double-Digit Net Income Growth Across All Four Segments; Broad-Based Earnings Improvement

    2Q26 net income of $9.1 billion (+27% YoY) and diluted EPS of $1.21 (+34% YoY), with total revenue up 15% YoY to $31.6 billion. Management noted that every business segment reported double-digit net income growth. ROTCE rose to 17.0% and ROA reached 1.03%, reflecting simultaneous gains in earnings breadth and returns.

  • 2.
    NII Grinding Higher; Deposit and Loan Momentum Continues

    Net interest income of $16.0 billion ($16.2 billion FTE) rose 9% YoY, driven by Global Markets activity-related NII, loan and deposit growth, and fixed-rate asset repricing, partially offset by the impact of lower interest rates. Average deposits of $2.02 trillion marked a 12th consecutive quarter of sequential growth, while average loans of $1.22 trillion grew 8% YoY, a 9th consecutive quarter of sequential growth.

  • 3.
    Standout Markets Businesses: IB Fees +50%, Equities Trading +70%

    Global Markets revenue rose 34% YoY to $8.0 billion. Sales and trading revenue of $7.1 billion (+33% YoY) marked a 17th consecutive quarter of YoY growth, with Equities surging 70% to $3.6 billion. Firmwide investment banking fees (excl. self-led) of $2.1 billion rose 50% YoY, and management noted the near-term deal pipeline remains strong.

  • 4.
    6.6% Positive Operating Leverage and Solid Capital; $8.0 Billion Returned to Shareholders

    Noninterest expense rose 8% YoY, well below revenue growth, driving 6.6% positive operating leverage and a 359 bps YoY improvement in the efficiency ratio to 59%. The net charge-off ratio of 0.47% declined 8 bps YoY, reflecting solid credit quality. The standardized CET1 ratio of 11.2% stands roughly 120 bps above the current 10.0% regulatory minimum, and the company returned $8.0 billion to shareholders via dividends and buybacks during the quarter, with tangible book value per share up 7% YoY to $29.37.

IV. Analyst Views

Morgan Stanley

Overweight
  • Broad-Based Earnings Improvement (PT $67.00): 2Q26 diluted EPS of $1.21 rose 34% YoY with revenue up 15%; all four segments posted double-digit net income growth and ROTCE climbed to 17.0%, combining earnings breadth with return quality.
  • Marked Improvement in Positive Operating Leverage: Revenue growth (+15%) far outpaced expense growth (+8%), delivering 6.6% operating leverage and a 359 bps YoY improvement in the efficiency ratio to 59%; cost discipline is supportive of better returns.

JP Morgan

Overweight
  • Steady NII Trajectory (PT $62.50): Net interest income of $16.0 billion ($16.2 billion FTE) rose 9% YoY on loan and deposit growth and fixed-rate asset repricing; average deposits grew for a 12th straight quarter and average loans for a 9th, sustaining balance-sheet momentum.
  • Resilient Credit Quality: The net charge-off ratio of 0.47% fell 8 bps YoY, the credit card loss rate declined from 3.82% to 3.55%, and the $1.4 billion provision was below the prior year's $1.6 billion; overall asset quality remains stable.

UBS

Buy
  • Standout Markets Momentum (PT $68.00): Global Markets revenue rose 34% YoY, Equities trading surged 70% to $3.6 billion, and sales and trading posted a 17th consecutive quarter of YoY growth; firmwide IB fees rose 50% YoY to $2.1 billion amid markedly improved capital markets activity.
  • Ample Capital Supports Shareholder Returns: The 11.2% CET1 ratio stands roughly 120 bps above the current 10.0% regulatory minimum; the company returned $8.0 billion to shareholders in the quarter (including $6.0 billion of buybacks), with TBVPS up 7% YoY to $29.37.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Interest Rate Path & NII Sensitivity Risk: NII growth this quarter was already partially offset by the impact of lower interest rates. Deeper- or faster-than-expected Fed rate cuts, or deviations in banking-book NII sensitivity assumptions, could compress the NII trajectory and earning-asset yields (FTE net interest yield on earning assets of 2.08% this quarter).
  2. High-Base Risk in Market-Sensitive Revenues: Equities trading (+70% YoY) and investment banking fees (+50% YoY) are highly sensitive to capital markets activity; a pullback in trading volumes or issuance would make this quarter's elevated base a significant drag on subsequent YoY momentum.
  3. Credit Cycle & Macroeconomic Assumption Shifts: The company's forward-looking statements caution that future credit losses may exceed current expectations due to changes in economic assumptions including unemployment, real estate prices, GDP, and corporate credit spreads. Commercial net charge-offs rose $22 million QoQ to $372 million, though still down $94 million YoY; overall commercial credit performance remains stable but warrants continued monitoring of corporate credit and macro conditions.
  4. Regulatory Capital & Legal/Compliance Uncertainty: Regulatory capital requirements, including the stress capital buffer and the G-SIB surcharge, remain subject to change. The company continues to face various legal and regulatory matters, including unemployment benefits processing and electronic payments/Zelle-related fraud; these cases are at various stages, and their ultimate financial and reputational impact remains uncertain.
