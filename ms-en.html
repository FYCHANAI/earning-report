I. Our Views

Based on 2Q 2026 earnings results and related disclosure documents, the following are the core conclusions distilled by the NTAM research team on Morgan Stanley (MS):

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Records Across Revenues, EPS and Pre-Tax Income; Profitability Steps Up Sharply

    2Q26 net revenues of $21.35 billion (+27% YoY), diluted EPS of $3.46 (+62% YoY) and pre-tax income of $7.35 billion all set quarterly records. ROTCE rose to 26.6% from 18.2% a year ago, while the efficiency ratio improved 6 percentage points to 65%, evidencing highly efficient conversion of revenue growth into earnings.

  • 2.
    Institutional Securities as the Largest Growth Engine; Record Equity Revenues

    Institutional Securities net revenues reached a record $11.04 billion (+44% YoY), with pre-tax income doubling to $4.26 billion. Equity net revenues hit an all-time high of $6.30 billion (+69% YoY) and Investment Banking revenues grew 58%, reflecting a broad-based recovery in client activity, M&A and underwriting markets.

  • 3.
    Record Wealth Management Net New Assets; Client Assets Surpass the $10 Trillion Milestone

    Wealth Management net revenues were a record $8.86 billion (+14% YoY), with net new assets of $148.1 billion setting a quarterly record (more than half driven by inflows related to a significant IPO in the Workplace channel) at a 30.5% pre-tax margin. Combined Wealth and Investment Management client assets surpassed $10 trillion.

  • 4.
    Ample Capital; Dividend and Buyback Authorization Both Upgraded

    The Standardized CET1 ratio stood at 14.8%, providing ample capital buffers. The Board announced a $0.15 increase in the quarterly dividend to $1.15 per share and reauthorized a share repurchase program of up to $20 billion beginning in Q3 2026, further strengthening capital returns.

V. Risk Factors

  1. High Capital-Markets Dependence and a High Base: Institutional Securities accounted for over half of quarterly net revenues, and record equities and investment banking results are closely tied to market activity. If M&A, underwriting or trading volumes normalize from elevated levels, momentum may prove hard to sustain off such a high base.
  2. Sustainability of Net New Assets: More than half of the record $148.1 billion in net new assets came from inflows tied to a single large IPO in the Workplace channel — an event-driven boost. A normalization of inflow levels in coming quarters should be factored into expectations.
  3. Valuation Premium Narrows the Margin of Safety: At a 16.96x forward P/E (Next 4 Qtrs Est), MS ranks near the top of U.S. large-cap bank peers, and the implied return to the ANR consensus target is only about 1.5%. Any disappointment versus optimistic expectations could trigger meaningful multiple compression.
  4. Rate and Deposit-Cost Dynamics: Wealth Management net interest income grew 18% YoY, benefiting from prevailing rate levels and deposit mix. A shift in the rate path or accelerated client deposit migration would compress this stable revenue driver.
