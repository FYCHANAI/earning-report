I. Our Views

Updated 2026/10/30. Q3 2026 ended September 26, 2026; results released October 29. Market inputs follow the dates in the supplied Bloomberg screenshots.

🎯

Key Operating Conclusions

  • 1.
    Server demand supports recovery; delivery determines durability

    DCAI revenue rose 77.8% to US$7.32bn and operating income reached US$3.04bn, offsetting weaker client demand. We believe server demand provides a firmer foundation for Intel’s recovery, but sustained revenue growth depends on Xeon supply, product competitiveness and execution. The next test is whether data-center momentum can continue against a higher comparison base.

  • 2.
    Softer Q4 guidance leaves earnings expectations vulnerable

    Management guides to Q4 revenue of US$15–16bn and non-GAAP EPS of US$0.25, below the supplied Bloomberg consensus of US$16.901bn and US$0.412. Non-GAAP gross-margin guidance also falls to 38.0%. We believe PC weakness and capacity allocation make the recovery uneven; a third-quarter earnings beat does not remove the risk of near-term earnings revisions.

  • 3.
    Foundry losses narrow, but commercial returns remain unproven

    Intel Foundry’s operating loss narrowed from US$2.32bn to US$1.85bn, still absorbing a substantial portion of product profits. Foundry revenue includes internal transactions and is not a direct measure of external customer growth. We believe process milestones must translate into better yields, cost efficiency and substantial external orders to support lasting valuation gains.

  • 4.
    Valuation anticipates recovery; cash and per-share returns matter

    Intel trades at 49.58x next-four-quarter earnings, close to AMD and well above the supplied NVDA and TSM multiples. Q3 adjusted free cash flow was US$1.64bn, and the company completed a US$23bn public equity offering. We believe the additional capital increases investment flexibility, but a larger share base and elevated valuation raise the requirement for sustainable per-share earnings and cash returns.

V. Risk Factors

  1. PC demand and supply constraints：Weak client demand, memory or substrate shortages, and server delivery constraints may affect revenue and gross margin.
  2. Process and foundry execution：Disappointing yields, production schedules, costs or external customer commitments could prolong Foundry losses.
  3. Capital intensity and dilution：Fab investment and working capital require substantial funding. New equity or financing supports liquidity but may constrain per-share returns; adjusted free cash flow also depends on incentives and partner funding.
  4. Competition, policy and earnings volatility：AMD, Arm-based products and custom chips, export controls, government policy and non-operating valuation changes can affect earnings and valuation.
