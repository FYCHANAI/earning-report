I. Our Views

Updated: 2026/10/22. Quarter ended 2026/09/30; results released 2026/10/21. Market-data dates are identified separately.

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Advertising volume and pricing monetize AI through the core business

    Q3 revenue rose 26.1% to US$64.601bn, with ad impressions up 10% and average ad pricing up 14%. September average daily active people reached 3.64bn. We believe improved recommendations and advertising tools support monetization beyond user growth alone. Sustained advertiser returns and engagement gains are essential to absorb the rising cost of AI.

  • 2.
    Litigation depresses reported earnings; operating efficiency still matters

    A US$9.89bn youth-related litigation charge reduced Q3 GAAP operating income to US$12.988bn and margin to 20%. The company disclosed operating income of approximately US$22.88bn and EPS of US$7.40 excluding this charge. We believe advertising growth still supports earnings, but research and infrastructure costs constrain operating leverage; adding back a special charge is not itself evidence of improving efficiency.

  • 3.
    Capital spending exceeds operating cash flow

    Q3 operating cash flow of US$32.786bn was below US$34.906bn of capex including finance-lease principal, producing negative US$2.120bn free cash flow. Full-year capex guidance narrowed to US$135–145bn. We believe substantial cash and securities provide an investment buffer, but new AI products must demonstrate incremental revenue and capital efficiency to prevent sustained cash consumption.

  • 4.
    Q4 outlook supports growth; valuation depends on investment returns

    Management guides Q4 revenue of US$73–76bn and expects Reality Labs losses to narrow to around US$15bn in 2027. The FY2027 GAAP+ P/E is 20.62x; the US$819.27 ANR target implies 17.5% upside from the US$697.52 reference price. We believe core advertising growth and improving loss-making operations are the main catalysts, while valuation upside must remain proportionate to the pace of AI investment returns and regulatory costs.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Legal and regulatory costs: Youth safety, privacy and advertising rules remain under scrutiny. The US$9.89bn quarterly charge does not establish that all related cash payments or other potential liabilities are settled.
  2. AI returns and cash flow: Heavy capital spending increases depreciation and financing needs. Slower monetization of new models and products could prolong free-cash-flow pressure.
  3. Advertising and competition: Advertising depends on customer returns, consumer conditions and platform engagement. Changes in recommendations, data signals or competition could weaken volume and pricing growth.
  4. New-business losses and capital structure: Reality Labs remains substantially loss-making. Proposed non-voting Class C shares add financing and employee-compensation flexibility, with potential dilution and governance implications; stock-dividend record and payment dates are not yet set.
