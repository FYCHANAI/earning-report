I. Our Views

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    AI Now Drives the Core Business; Revenue Growth Back in High Gear

    Q2 2026 total revenue reached $60,801 million (GAAP, +28% YoY; +27% YoY on a constant-currency basis, Non-GAAP), powered by +14% ad impressions and +12% average price per ad. The company disclosed that LLMs are now deeply embedded in its recommendation and ads systems: GEM and sequence-learning models lifted Facebook ad clicks by 8.3% and conversions by 15.7%; Family of Apps other revenue reached $1 billion for the first time (+73% YoY), driven mainly by WhatsApp paid messaging and subscriptions. We believe AI's contribution to monetization efficiency has moved from narrative to quantifiable.

  • 2.
    One-Off Items Compressed Margins; Adjusted Fundamentals Remain Solid

    Operating income was $18,775 million (GAAP, -8% YoY) with a 31% operating margin (vs. 43% a year ago), mainly reflecting a $2.40 billion legal charge and $1.18 billion of severance recorded this quarter. Management stated on the earnings call that operating income would have grown about 9% YoY excluding these two items (a management-adjusted view, not a GAAP-reported item). We believe the core advertising profit engine has not deteriorated, though expense digestion requires quarter-by-quarter verification.

  • 3.
    Capex Surged to $31.08 Billion; Free Cash Flow Under Pressure

    Quarterly capital expenditures (including principal payments on finance leases) reached $31,078 million, up about 83% YoY, driving free cash flow down to $784 million (Non-GAAP). The company narrowed FY2026 capex guidance to $130–145 billion, issued a net $24.91 billion of long-term debt during the quarter, and announced a 1GW data-center partnership with BlackRock. We believe investors must weigh long-term AI returns against near-term cash consumption, and rising debt leverage warrants continued monitoring.

  • 4.
    Valuation Offers Upside, With a Caveat on Peer-Multiple Bases

    Based on the 07/29/2026 closing price of $585.61, META offers roughly 36.7% upside to the Bloomberg ANR consensus target of $800.36. Per the bases shown on the Bloomberg EEO screen, META's 18.3x forward P/E is the lowest among the four mega-cap peers on this page (META, GOOGL and AMZN on GAAP EPS; MSFT on Adjusted EPS — cross-company multiples are for directional comparison only). We believe valuation remains attractive against Q3 revenue guidance of $61–64 billion.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Litigation & Regulatory Risk: A $2.40 billion legal charge was recorded this quarter; the company disclosed it continues to face scrutiny over youth-related issues across multiple markets, with several US youth-related trials scheduled this year that could ultimately result in material losses.
  2. Elevated Capex & Free-Cash-Flow Pressure: FY2026 capex (including finance leases) guidance stands at $130–145 billion while quarterly free cash flow was just $784 million (Non-GAAP); long-term debt rose to $83.66 billion (net long-term debt issuance of $24.91 billion in the quarter), making payback cadence and funding costs key variables.
  3. Expenses Growing Faster Than Revenue: Total costs and expenses rose 55% YoY this quarter (including one-off items) and FY total-expense guidance is $165–169 billion; compensation (notably AI talent), infrastructure depreciation and third-party compute costs keep climbing, leaving margin recovery uncertain.
  4. Macro, FX & Geopolitical Risk: Q3 guidance assumes an FX headwind of about 1% to YoY revenue growth; the company disclosed Q1 2026 DAP was affected by the internet shutdown in Iran and Russia's restrictions on WhatsApp, keeping regional access restrictions an external variable for the user base.
