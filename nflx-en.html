I. Our Views

Updated: 2026/10/21. The quarter ended 2026/09/30, with results released on 2026/10/20. Based on Netflix’s Q3 shareholder letter and financial statements; company actuals, guidance and Bloomberg market forecasts are presented separately.

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Resilient Subscriptions Support the Full-Year Earnings Trend

    We believe member retention and pricing power remain the foundations of Netflix’s profitability. Q3 revenue of US$12.874bn rose 11.8% YoY, while the 34.6% operating margin exceeded the company’s original 33.2% forecast. The outperformance primarily reflected expense timing, however, and the prior-year quarter included a US$619m Brazilian tax charge. Unchanged full-year margin guidance of 31.5%, up 2 percentage points from 2025, supports continued earnings improvement but does not signal a marked acceleration in growth.

  • 2.
    Slower Advertising Monetization Shifts Focus to 2027 Expansion

    Full-year advertising revenue expectations fell from approximately US$3.0bn to US$2.7bn because some advertising product capabilities are rolling out more slowly than expected; subscription revenue performed better than the company expected. We believe a near-term valuation recovery requires stronger advertising execution alongside ad-tier membership growth. Plans to enter 15 additional advertising markets in 2027, supported by programmatic buying and measurement capabilities, offer medium-term growth potential. Converting available advertising inventory into revenue remains the key test.

  • 3.
    Cash Generation Supports Buybacks, with Capital Discipline Still Essential

    Q3 free cash flow was US$2.884bn, up 8.4% YoY, versus approximately US$4.8bn of buybacks. Quarter-end cash was approximately US$8.2bn, with US$22.3bn of repurchase authorization remaining. We believe buybacks continue to support earnings per share, but quarterly repurchases exceeded free cash flow; their sustained contribution depends on recurring cash generation and balance-sheet capacity. Full-year free cash flow guidance fell to US$12.0bn, mainly because of a Q4 real-estate transaction. Full-year free cash flow also benefits from the after-tax contribution of the US$2.8bn Warner Bros. termination fee received in Q1, while sustained repurchase capacity rests on cash generated by the core business.

  • 4.
    Valuation Looks Attractive; Re-Rating Requires Faster Growth

    At Bloomberg’s FY2027 GAAP+ P/E of 18.14x, we believe Netflix’s resilient subscription revenue and margin expansion offer medium-term appeal. The ANR consensus target of US$90.12 implies 31.2% upside from the 10/20 reference price of US$68.69. Revenue growth has nevertheless slowed from 16.2% in Q1 to 11.8% in Q3. A re-rating still requires advertising product delivery, stronger viewing and returns on content investment. Q4 live programming and the 2027 advertising-market expansion are key developments to monitor; the current valuation offers medium-term return potential, but execution risk constrains near-term re-rating until advertising and viewing growth recover.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Advertising Product Delivery and Demand: The reduction in advertising revenue expectations reflects delayed product capabilities. If new features, measurement tools or additional markets fail to attract advertising budgets, revenue growth and a valuation recovery may take longer. Advertising spending is also sensitive to economic conditions.
  2. Content Returns, Competition and Retention: Streaming, free video and other entertainment platforms compete for viewing time. If new series, live programming and licensed content fail to improve engagement or retention, pricing flexibility may narrow while content and rights costs remain elevated.
  3. Quarterly Margin and Cash-Flow Volatility: Expense timing benefited Q3, while Q4 guidance includes headcount-reduction costs; the 34.6% quarterly margin should not be treated as a sustainable run rate. Real-estate spending and content payments can affect cash flow, and the termination fee included in the full-year figure is not an annually recurring cash source.
  4. Foreign Exchange, Regulation and Capital Allocation: International operations expose revenue and profit to currency movements, while local content, tax and data rules may raise costs. Repurchases persistently exceeding recurring free cash flow, or additional large investments, could constrain financial flexibility and per-share returns.
