I. Our Views

Updated: 2026/10/08. Based on the FQ4 / FY2026 results for the period ended 2026/09/03, released on 2026/09/30, and the company presentation. The following is NTAM analysis; market consensus is separately sourced and dated.

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Data Centers Drive Profitability; Full-Year Revenue Rises 256.3%

    FQ4 revenue reached US$54.229bn, up 379.3% YoY and 30.8% QoQ, with a non-GAAP operating margin of 82.3%. CMBU and CDBU generated US$34.285bn, or 63.2% of revenue; full-year revenue was US$133.188bn. 1-gamma DRAM and G9 NAND are now the largest production nodes for their respective technologies. We believe the key question is whether data-center demand can support additional supply and sustain these high margins.

  • 2.
    26 SCAs Improve Visibility, but Pricing Protection Has Limits

    The company has 26 strategic customer agreements covering over 35% of estimated revenue through 2030. Around three-quarters of estimated SCA revenue has a defined pricing framework, mostly with price floors and ceilings. Approximately US$150bn of RPO includes only committed quantities at minimum prices under contracts with pricing frameworks; US$32bn of customer financial commitments is not cash already received. We believe long-term agreements improve demand visibility but do not lock in all revenue or profit.

  • 3.
    Strong Cash Conversion as Capacity Investment Enters Its Next Phase

    FQ4 operating cash flow was US$43.973bn. After US$10.774bn of net capital expenditure, adjusted free cash flow was US$33.199bn, or 61.2% of revenue. Bloomberg displays FCF of US$32.863bn. The US$336m difference from the company measure equals US$9m in equipment-sale proceeds plus US$327m in government incentives. This page uses the company-adjusted measure. Quarterly SCA customer deposits of approximately US$12.3bn are classified as financing cash flow. The company expects net capex of about US$11.5bn in FY27 Q1, about US$25bn in the first half and more in the second half, without an exact full-year amount.

  • 4.
    FQ1-27 Guidance Signals Further Growth; Low P/E Still Requires Durable Earnings

    The company guides FQ1-27 revenue of US$61.5bn ± US$1.5bn, non-GAAP EPS of US$38.15 ± US$1.00 and non-GAAP gross margin of approximately 86.25%. Management expects this to be the FY27 gross-margin low, with improvement thereafter. Bloomberg shows MU at 6.27x next-four-quarter P/E, above the three peers at 4.06–4.23x, although accounting bases and periods are not fully aligned. The ANR consensus target of US$1,621.44 implies 49.0% upside from the displayed 10/07 share price of US$1,088.00 (excluding dividends).

V. Risk Factors

  1. Memory Pricing and Elevated Earnings Expectations May Fall: NTAM believes slower AI investment, rising customer inventories or supply growth outpacing demand could lead to cuts in high gross margins and forward EPS estimates; a 6.27x forward P/E does not guarantee valuation protection.
  2. Advanced-Node and Capacity-Ramp Execution Risk: Yields, packaging and delivery progress for 1-gamma, G9 NAND and HBM will affect cost per bit and customer supply. Management expects first wafer output from ID1 in mid-2027; volume contributions still require a ramp, so initial production must not be equated with immediate full capacity.
  3. SCA Pricing and Customer Concentration Risk: Long-term agreements do not cover all expected revenue, and some prices are periodically renegotiated against the market. Most contracts with pricing frameworks have floors and ceilings, rather than entirely fixed prices. US$150bn of RPO and US$32bn of financial commitments must not be treated as current revenue or cash already received; deliveries, contractual performance and customer credit still require monitoring.
  4. Cash Requirements for Expansion and External Risks: Net capex is expected to be about US$25bn in the first half of FY27 and higher in the second half. Delays in construction, equipment or incentive receipts could pressure cash conversion; trade restrictions, geopolitical developments and supply-chain changes could also disrupt deliveries. These are NTAM risk assessments.
