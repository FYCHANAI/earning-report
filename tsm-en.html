I. Our Views

Updated: 2026/10/16. Based on TSMC’s third-quarter results for the period ended 2026/09/30, released on 2026/10/15, and management’s outlook.

🎯

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    HPC Growth Sustains Strong Core Profitability

    We believe TSMC is a key beneficiary of the shift toward high-performance computing, with its advanced-node advantage translating into tangible earnings growth. Q3 USD revenue grew 41.2% YoY, while HPC revenue rose 22% QoQ and accounted for 68% of total revenue. Operating margin increased 8.1 percentage points YoY to 58.7%. Demand growth alongside improving profitability supports our positive view of the core business outlook.

  • 2.
    Growing 2nm Contribution and High Margins Support the Next Growth Phase

    2nm’s share of wafer revenue rose from 3% in the previous quarter to 8%. The company’s Q4 revenue guidance midpoint of US$53.3bn implies approximately 14.1% sequential growth, with gross margin expected to remain at 65–67%. We believe advanced-node migration can extend TSMC’s growth cycle. While the initial 2nm production ramp adds costs, the high gross-margin guidance signals resilience in overall profitability. Continued improvements in yields and capacity utilization would provide additional support for future earnings growth.

  • 3.
    Strong Cash Generation Funds Expansion and Shareholder Returns

    Q3 operating cash flow reached NT$919.77bn, approximately 2.1 times capital expenditure. After investing NT$443.21bn, TSMC still generated NT$476.56bn of free cash flow. We believe this cash-generating capacity is an important advantage during a period of heavy capital spending. It allows TSMC to fund technology upgrades and capacity expansion internally, provides a financial foundation for continued dividends and reduces reliance on external financing.

  • 4.
    Earnings Growth Supports Medium-Term Value, with the 2nm Ramp in Focus

    We believe a forecast FY2027 P/E of 18.74 times, together with advanced-node growth and strong cash flow, supports TSMC’s medium-term investment value. A growing 2nm revenue contribution and sustained high gross margins are potential catalysts for upward earnings revisions. The Bloomberg consensus target of US$573.07 is approximately 35.7% above the 10/15 reference share price of US$422.25. Our positive view depends on continued delivery of earnings growth; the key risks are slower customer capital spending and a 2nm production ramp that falls short of expectations.

V. Risk Factors

  1. AI Investment Cycle and Customer Concentration: HPC accounts for 68% of net revenue and North American customers for 79%, but HPC is not equivalent to AI revenue, and customer location does not indicate production location. We believe slower AI or data-center investment by major customers could pressure orders and earnings forecasts; an 18.74x forward P/E does not guarantee valuation protection.
  2. N2 Ramp and Overseas Expansion Execution: The initial N2 ramp has affected Q3 gross margin. Overseas fab construction, yields and cost improvements may continue to affect profitability and cash returns. Nine-month capex of US$40.64bn is not a full-year budget and does not establish that new capacity is fully utilized.
  3. Currency Translation and Margin Volatility: Q4 guidance assumes USD/NTD of 31.8. Currency movements away from that assumption may change reported revenue and margins. This quarter’s 50.9% NTD revenue growth and 41.2% USD growth are distinct measures; currency also affects the difference between ordinary-share and USD ADR EPS growth.
  4. Export Restrictions, Geopolitics and Supply Chains: Export controls, trade policy and geopolitical events may affect customer demand, equipment availability and cross-border deliveries. Geographic capacity expansion adds flexibility but cannot eliminate production concentration or supply-chain disruption risks.
