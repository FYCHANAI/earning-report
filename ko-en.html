I. Our Views

Core Operational Conclusions

  • 1.
    Volume-led growth supports the upgraded outlook

    We believe Coca-Cola’s shift toward volume-led growth supports medium-term earnings resilience. Q3 net revenue was US$13.380bn, up 7%; organic revenue grew 7% and global unit case volume rose 5%, with Coca-Cola Zero Sugar up 16%. India, China, Mexico and Brazil led growth, while North American volume was flat. Concentrate shipments outpaced unit case volume by 2 percentage points, making sustained consumer demand the next checkpoint.

  • 2.
    Core margins improve steadily, with additional currency support

    Comparable operating margin reached 32.93%, up 106 basis points. Expansion was 46 basis points at constant currency and 21 basis points after also excluding acquisitions and divestitures. We believe currency was an important contributor to this quarter’s margin expansion, while underlying operating improvement was more modest. Comparable EPS of US$0.91 rose 11%, including a roughly 2-point currency benefit; higher marketing and operating expenses remain important constraints.

  • 3.
    Cash generation supports dividends and brand investment

    Nine-month operating cash flow of US$12.471bn less US$1.063bn of capex generated US$11.408bn of free cash flow. Full-year FCF guidance increased from about US$12.4bn to at least US$13.0bn. We believe this supports dividends and reinvestment. Last year’s US$6.1bn one-off fairlife contingent consideration payment lowered the comparison base, while the higher full-year FCF guidance reinforces our view that cash generation is improving.

  • 4.
    A premium valuation leaves more moderate upside

    FY2027 Adj+ P/E is 25.87x versus 13.61–17.76x for PepsiCo (PEP), Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP) and Nestlé. The ANR consensus target of US$97.80 implies 7.3% price upside from the 27 October reference price of US$91.18, excluding dividends. We believe brand and cash-flow quality support a premium, but further rerating depends on delivering at least 5% full-year organic growth and at least 11% comparable EPS growth. The company’s preliminary 2027 considerations indicate that acquisitions and divestitures will create roughly 8% and 2% headwinds to comparable net revenue and comparable EPS, respectively, weighing on reported growth as the business mix changes.

V. Risk Factors

  1. Consumer demand and shipment timing: North American volume was flat and concentrate shipments outpaced consumer unit case volume. Weaker demand or a reversal in shipment timing could slow organic growth. Health preferences and sugary-drink regulation may also affect mix.
  2. Costs, marketing and price/mix: Higher marketing investment and operating expenses partly offset revenue growth, while Asia Pacific price/mix fell 2%. Rising costs combined with weaker pricing power could limit further margin improvement.
  3. Currency and valuation premium: Q3 comparable EPS growth included roughly 2 points of currency benefit, while early 2027 considerations indicate a slight headwind. At 25.87x FY2027 P/E, below-plan earnings could pressure both profit forecasts and the premium to peers.
  4. Tax litigation and divestiture execution: The IRS dispute remains unresolved; the 19.7% full-year underlying tax rate excludes an adverse outcome. The African bottling sale is expected to close in Q4, subject to regulatory approval and closing timing.
